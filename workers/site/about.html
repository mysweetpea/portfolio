00 / THE SHORT VERSION

What MySweetPea actually is.

MySweetPea is one person's home server — a real machine, running nine free, open-source apps that big companies normally run in exchange for your data. There is no company behind it, no startup, no investors — just a gardener who got tired of being the product.

You get one account and use the services like any app you already know: photos back up like Google Photos, movies stream like Netflix, files sync like Google Drive, passwords like 1Password, chat like WhatsApp, notes like Notion. The names change — the deal behind them does not: nothing here is funded by advertising, so there is nothing to sell. No profile on you, no feed shaped by your data, no price that changes once you're locked in.

Membership is a single one-time contribution that keeps the lights on — that is the entire business model. And if you ever leave, everything exports in standard formats. The garden is easy to visit and easy to leave; that is the point.

P·00

Self-hosting, in plain terms

It means running internet services on your own hardware instead of renting them from a big cloud company. Your photo library lives on a computer you could open up and touch — not in an ad-funded datacenter that reads it for a living. Every service on this site works that way.

01 / ORIGIN

How the garden started

3 beats

MySweetPea started in May 2026, out of frustration. It felt like every big company was collecting more and more of our personal information — being anonymous online was basically disappearing. The breaking point for me was the new age-verification laws: governments requiring you to prove who you are just to browse normally, with those checks even being built into the devices themselves. I didn't want to accept that as normal.

So I did something about it — I started running my own services on my own computer, in my own home. The tech world calls this "self-hosting": instead of your photos, files or passwords living on some company's machines, they live on hardware you own. I expected it to feel like a chore. Instead, I loved it: services I control completely — no ads, no tracking, no subscriptions. And pretty quickly I wanted to share it. Plenty of people are sick of the same things I am — so the garden exists for them. I built a proper server so it could reach beyond just me, run by one person, kept for everyone who uses it.

Everything you can use on MySweetPea runs on my own hardware — nothing is rented from a big cloud company. It's built for people first: a place that answers to its members, not to an advertiser or an algorithm. I started with spare parts I already had and slowly added more as it grew, enough to run nine services, tended daily. And honestly? I just really enjoy this — tinkering with the server, improving it, watching it grow. That's probably the most true thing about this place: it's run by one person who actually loves running it, for the people who use it.

02 / PRINCIPLES

What “self-hosted” means here

In plain terms: almost everything online runs on someone else's computer, under someone else's rules. Not here. Every service in this garden runs on my own hardware, at home — and that one difference changes everything about how your data is treated:

P·01

You hold the keys

Your files, photos and passwords live on hardware in this garden — not replicated into someone's ad warehouse.

P·02

Work, not magic

No dark patterns, no lock-in. Updates, backups and fixes done by hand, on purpose.

P·03

No third parties

No brokers, no trackers, no “partners.” There is no one to sell your activity to.

P·04

Transparent by design

Open-source services, public infrastructure code. You can read exactly how this place runs.
