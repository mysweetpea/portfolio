00 Before anything else

            
What MySweetPea actually is.

            

                

                    
MySweetPea is one person's home server — a real machine, in a real house, running nine free, open-source apps that big companies normally run in exchange for your data.

                    
You get an account, sign in once, and use them like any app: photos back up like Google Photos, movies stream like Netflix, passwords sync like 1Password. The difference is what happens behind the screen: nothing here is funded by advertising, so there is nothing to sell — no profile on you, no feed shaped by your data, no price that changes the moment you're locked in.

                    
membership = a one-time contribution that keeps the lights on. that's the entire business model.

                

                

                    
In plain terms

                    
Self-hosting means running internet services on your own hardware instead of renting them from a big cloud company. Think: your photo library living on a computer you could open up and touch — not in an ad-funded datacenter that reads it for a living.

                    

                        
it is
9 apps, 1 login — photos, movies, files, passwords, chat, notes

                        
it is not
a company, a startup, or a free tier with a catch

                        
you pay
one time, then everything is included — forever

                        
you can leave
anytime — standard-format export, no lock-in

01 The Garden

            
Each one replaces something that watches you.

            
Same comfort, same polish — different incentives. Open any card to see what its corporate twin does with the same data.

            

                

                    
                    

                        

                            
The alternative, exposed

                            
Netflix records every play, pause, rewind and abandon, builds a behavioral profile per household, and uses it to decide what you're shown — and on its ad tier, your viewing history feeds behavioral advertising profiles. Meanwhile you rent, never own: titles vanish when licences expire, and prices climb yearly.

                            
Jellyfin streams your files, records nothing, and answers to no ad network. Nobody can take a title out of your own library.

                            
Sources: Netflix: behavioral ads opt-out · Netflix ads partner announcement

                        

                    

                

                

                    
                    

                        

                            
The alternative, exposed

                            
Every document, calendar entry and contact lives in Google's datacenters, where automated systems scan content for policy violations — and Google holds the encryption keys, not you. People have been locked out of their own files by false flags, with a review process that has no human to appeal to. Your calendar knows where you'll be Tuesday. Drive knows what you're writing.

                            
Nextcloud here means your files live on my disk, sync is verifiable, and there's no terms-of-service lawyer between you and your own documents.

                            
Sources: Google: flagged-file review · Drive is not zero-knowledge

                        

                    

                

                

                    
                    

                        

                            
The alternative, exposed

                            
Google Photos scans your pictures on Google's servers: face models are computed in their cloud, on their terms. The 'free unlimited storage' that hooked everyone was revoked in 2021 — the photos were the bait, the subscription was always the plan. And as of 2026, media you upload to Google services is used to train AI unless you opt out.

                            
Here, your photos sit on my hardware, face recognition is optional and runs locally, and the only person who could ever look is me — and I'd need a reason you'd hear about.

                            
Sources: unlimited storage ended, 2021 · AI training unless opted out

                        

                    

                

                

                    
                    

                        

                            
The alternative, exposed

                            
WhatsApp messages are end-to-end encrypted — but Meta still collects who you talk to, how often, from where, and on what device, and merges it with your Instagram and Facebook activity into one advertising identity. In 2024, Meta began training its AI on user content in regions where no opt-out existed at all.

                            
Matrix here is encrypted by default, the server is mine, and the metadata stays in my database — not in Meta's social graph.

                            
Sources: WhatsApp privacy policy · Meta AI training, 2024

                        

                    

                

                

                    
                    

                        

                            
The alternative, exposed

                            
LastPass — a company whose entire product is keeping secrets — was breached, and encrypted vaults were stolen from its cloud storage; the fallout ended in a class-action settlement. 1Password is closed-source: you cannot inspect its crypto claims, you subscribe forever, and your vault lives in their cloud on their schedule.

                            
Vaultwarden is Bitwarden-compatible, open source, end-to-end encrypted — and the server is this one, the one whose uptime anyone can watch right now.

                            
Sources: LastPass breach · Bitwarden security model

                        

                    

                

                

                    
                    

                        

                            
The alternative, exposed

                            
Your workspace — plans, journals, maybe client material — sits on Notion's servers under terms that can change, with AI features that process your notes through third-party cloud models. Evernote showed what happens when your second brain belongs to a company that needs an exit: free plans capped at 50 notes, price hikes, exodus.

                            
AFFiNE here keeps notes on this server, exports to open formats, and no acquisition can relocate them.

                            
Sources: Notion AI processing · Evernote 50-note cap

                        

                    

                

                

                    
                    

                        

                            
The alternative, exposed

                            
Free-tier AI chats reserve the right to train on everything you type — your drafts, your questions, your mistakes. Shared conversations have surfaced in public search results; contractors review real chats for training; retention policies change under your feet.

                            
Here, chat history lives in my database, model choice is explicit, and nothing you type becomes training data without a decision made by a person, not a policy update.

                            
Sources: shared chats indexed · contractor chat review

                        

                    

                

                

                    
                    

                        

                            
The alternative, exposed

                            
Watch-party browser extensions ask for 'read and change all your data on the websites you visit' — a browser-wide permission for a play button. Whatever their intent, that's a technical capability, not a promise.

                            
KoalaSync is just a room on my server: no extension, no page permissions, no account anywhere else.

                            
Sources: what the permission grants

                        

                    

                

                

                    
                    

                        

                            
The alternative, exposed

                            
Streaming catalogs log every browse to predict — and steer — what you'll watch next. Your taste is the product that keeps you paying.

                            
Seerr works with your library, not a profile of you. Requests go to me, not to a recommendation engine.

05 Questions

            
Asked & answered.

            

                
Why is access invite-only?+
Capacity is real — hardware, bandwidth and one gardener. Invites keep the garden fast and supported for the people already in it. When capacity grows, access opens.

                
What happens to my data if this disappears?+
Every service exports in standard formats — files, calendars, photos, passwords. No lock-in is a design rule here, not a promise.

                
Is it really free of tracking?+
No analytics, no trackers, no ad networks. The only public metrics are the uptime monitors on the status page.

                
Can I request a new service?+
Yes — that is the roadmap. Members suggest, the garden grows. Use the Suggest a Service link in the footer.

                
What does it cost?+
A one-time community contribution — amount confirmed in your invite email. It covers power, hardware, bandwidth and the domain. No subscription, no renewal, ever.
